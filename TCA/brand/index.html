01 — Foundation

Four pillars, one system

Everything downstream in this book — the colours, the type, the photography, the tone — earns its place by serving one of these four. If an element doesn't trace back to a pillar, it doesn't belong in the brand.

Confidence

Belief that's trained, not assumed. We speak to people as capable, and design for the moment they prove it to themselves.

Self-belief, earned

Community

Progress witnessed, not solitary. Every member trains alongside others who want them to succeed.

Belonging, shared

Accessibility

Low barriers to entry, in price, in language, in physical and digital design. Confidence can't be exclusive and mean anything.

Open, by design

Action

The pillar that turns the other three into results — and the pillar our tagline speaks from directly: Get Britain Moving.

Momentum, always

How the pillars relate

They're a loop, not a list: Confidence prompts Action; Action is easier to take inside a Community; a Community only works if it's Accessible to everyone in it; and Accessibility — being able to walk in and belong — is what builds Confidence in the first place. "Get Britain Moving" is the whole loop compressed into three words — everyone, moving, together.

02 — Positioning

Where this sits in the market

The Gym Group leans into blue and green — value and grit. PureGym leans into cool teal-green — scale and convenience. Neither speaks from a national identity. Ours does: Royal Blue and Red aren't picked for taste, they're drawn directly from the Union Jack, in service of a tagline about the whole country moving.

What's genuinely ownable

A palette a returning member reads as unmistakably British before they read a word of copy. No UK fitness competitor is currently built on this association — it's ours to hold.

What anchors it

The logo itself — a runner in motion, rendered in Royal Blue and Red — already carries this identity. The rest of the system (colour, photography, tone) is built to match what the mark already says, not the other way round.

03 — Logo usage

One mark, used exactly as supplied

This is our registered mark. It is not redrawn, recoloured, or reinterpreted anywhere in this book — every version below is the same artwork, placed at different sizes on different backgrounds.

Primary lockup — runner mark, wordmark and tagline as one fixed unitSVG, vector-wrapped original artwork

Why this file isn't hand-traced

The SVG delivered with this book embeds the exact pixels of the artwork you supplied, rather than an automatically re-traced outline. Auto-tracing a mark this detailed — the motion lines, the gradient-shaded runner, the interlocking wordmark — risks subtly altering curves and edges. For a registered mark, exact reproduction matters more than file-format purity, so this SVG is a lossless vector wrapper around the original: infinitely embeddable, zero visual alteration.

Clear space & minimum size

Clear space

Minimum clear space on every side equals the height of the word "THE" at the top-left of the lockup. Nothing else — text, edges, other logos — enters that margin.

Minimum size

160px wide minimum for digital use, so the tagline stays legible. Below that, the wordmark and tagline blur into noise before the runner does.

Backgrounds

✓White, Cloud, or any light neutral background — this is what the artwork was built for, and where it has full contrast.
✕Never place it on Royal Blue, Red, navy, black, or any dark surface. The runner and the tagline are both rendered in dark navy — on a dark background they disappear almost entirely.
✕Never place it over busy photography without a solid light card or panel behind it.

What's not to be done, ever

✕Don't recolour, restyle, or apply the brand gradient to any part of the mark.
✕Don't separate the runner from the wordmark, or the tagline from the wordmark — they're supplied, and used, as one fixed unit.
✕Don't stretch, skew, rotate, or crop the artwork.
✕Don't re-set "Get Britain Moving" in a different typeface — the tagline as shown is part of the registered artwork, not a separate text element.

Known gaps — flagged, not filled

!No standalone icon-only version exists. The runner and wordmark overlap in the artwork, so a clean "icon alone" crop isn't available without redrawing — which we won't do to a registered mark. A true icon-only lockup should be commissioned from the original designer if one is needed for app icons or favicons.
!No reversed (light-on-dark) version exists. If the brand needs to appear on a dark surface — a stadium screen, a dark app theme — a light/white version should be produced by the original designer, not approximated here.

Until those versions exist, the safest UI substitute for a small square touchpoint (browser tab favicon, temporary app icon) is a plain "TCA" monogram — shown in the Mobile app section — clearly not presented as the trademark itself.

The signature gradient — updated to the primary colours

Highlight panels — hero banners, stat cards, feature tiles — use a 115° gradient from Royal Blue into Red. It never touches the logo itself, which stays exactly as supplied; it's reserved for UI moments the brand fully controls.

Accessibility — Ink on white: 17.8:1. White on Royal Blue: 13.5:1. White on Red: 5.5:1. All meet AA comfortably. Gold and Sky Blue are used as light tints with dark text, never as a solid fill behind white text.

06 — Photography

Candid, joyful, real — never staged

Photography is candid, joyful and real — never staged, never glossy. It prioritises diverse bodies, ages and backgrounds, and it never shows anyone standing still: everyone in frame is either moving or connecting with someone else. Lifestyle and natural imagery from Unsplash, shown below, sets the reference bar.

Candid, not staged

Caught in the moment, not directed for camera. If it looks like anyone was asked to "do that again but bigger," it's the wrong shot.

Confidence

Every body, every age, every background

Diverse representation is the rule for every gallery, not a single token image tucked in to check a box.

Accessibility

Joyful and in connection

People with people — eye contact, spotting, high-fives. Joy is visible on faces, not implied by a caption.

Community

Always in motion

Never passive, never posed standing still. Mid-rep, mid-stride, mid-laugh — always doing something, never just present for it.

Action

Colour treatment on photography

No duotone wash, no gradient. If a photo needs brand colour tying it to a page, use a single flat brand colour at low opacity (Royal Blue or Ink at 15–25%) as an even tint across the whole frame — one colour, one opacity, no blend.

What we avoid

Staged or overly polished "glossy" compositions, airbrushed or heavily retouched bodies, gym-selfie mirror shots, anyone standing passively for the camera, empty equipment with no one using it, and any gallery where only one body type, age or background appears.

07b — Text on imagery

Legibility first, always

Text over photography uses a flat solid scrim, not a gradient fade. A flat block guarantees contrast across its entire area; a fade guarantees it only at one end and quietly fails at the other.

Hierarchy, top to bottom

1Pillar tag — a flat-coloured badge, 11–12px, always present, identifies which pillar the content serves.
2Headline — Sora 700, 22–34px depending on context, maximum two lines.
3Supporting line — Inter 400, 14–16px, white at ~78% opacity, one line on cards, two maximum on a hero.
4CTA (optional) — a solid flat button or a white outline button; never text floating on the image without a button shape around it.

Legibility rules

✓Text sits only within the scrim or panel area — never directly on unmasked image detail.
✓Scrim is a flat fill at minimum 75% opacity, sized generously enough to guarantee 4.5:1 contrast for every word inside it.
✓Minimum safe margin: 24px on mobile, 40px on web, from every edge of the frame.
✓Headline text never exceeds ~75% of the frame width, keeping a visible margin of image on both sides.
✕Don't use a gradient fade as a scrim — swap it for a flat block at fixed opacity.
✕Don't centre-align long text over imagery — centring is reserved for single short tags only.

09 — Voice & tone

Four pillars, four habits of speech

Every piece of copy should be checkable against the pillar it's meant to serve. If a line doesn't do one of these four jobs, cut it.

Confidence

Direct, affirming, specific

Short sentences, active verbs, named progress. "You held that plank ten seconds longer than last week" beats "great effort today!"

Community

"We" and "together", not "you alone"

Coach, not drill sergeant. Credit the group as often as the individual: "The 6am crew hit a new team total this week."

Accessibility

Plain language, no jargon left unexplained

Write at a level that needs no fitness background to follow. Every class listing states modifications available, not just the advanced version.

Action

One clear next step, every time

Claims are specific and true, and end in a CTA the member can take now — "Book Tuesday's beginner circuit," in service of getting one more person moving.

12 — Accessibility standard

Not a footnote — a pillar

Because Accessibility is one of the four pillars, it gets enforced minimums here rather than being left to a component's default styling.

Visual

✓Body text never below 16px; line height 1.5 minimum.
✓Text and interactive elements hold 4.5:1 contrast against their background at minimum, including at both ends of the signature gradient, not just the average.
✓Colour is never the only signal — pillar badges pair colour with a label and an icon, not colour alone.

Interaction

✓Touch targets minimum 44×44px on mobile, with visible focus states on web for keyboard navigation.
✓All video content carries captions by default; alt text is written for every image, not left blank.
✓Every class format states its physical modifications in the listing itself, not one tap deeper.
