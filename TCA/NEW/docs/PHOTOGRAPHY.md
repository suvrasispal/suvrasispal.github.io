# Photography — source and licence record

**Visible photo credits have been removed from the site** at the client's
request. This file is the internal record of what each image is and where it
came from. Keep it updated when images change.

All nine photographs are free to use commercially under the
[Unsplash License](https://unsplash.com/license), which does **not** legally
require attribution — so removing the visible credits is within licence.
Unsplash does *request* credit as good practice, and photographers expect it,
so this is a business decision rather than a compliance one. Nothing here is
at legal risk.

Images are hot-linked from `images.unsplash.com`. Nothing is redistributed
in this package.

## Two things worth recording

**1. Credits were removed by instruction, not oversight.**
If TCA later wants to reinstate them — for a press pack, an awards entry, or
simply as good practice — the removed markup was a small `<p class="credit">`
inside each image frame. Re-adding it is straightforward.

**2. The photography no longer represents every age.**
The brief specified young people and excluded older adults. That is a
deliberate change of direction, and it sits against the brand book's
photography rule (§06): *"Diverse representation is the rule for every
gallery, not a single token image tucked in to check a box"*, and its
Accessibility pillar. The phrase "All ages" was removed from the site in the
same pass.

The remaining copy still says the sessions are for *everyone*, *all fitness
levels*, *beginners* and *people returning to exercise*. Worth a conscious
decision about whether the imagery and the words are telling the same story,
particularly for a Community Interest Company whose stated mission is
reaching people who feel invisible in ordinary fitness spaces.

Flagged for the record, not to reopen the decision.

## Before launch

The brand book is explicit (§07): *"All photography on this page is
lifestyle stock from Unsplash, shown for creative direction only — replace
with commissioned photography of real members before shipping any of this to
production."*

These images are correctly licensed and match the brief, but they show other
people's members. Treat them as a high-quality placeholder set that
establishes the standard a commissioned shoot should meet.
